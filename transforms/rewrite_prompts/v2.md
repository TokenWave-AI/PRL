## What a good rewrite fixes
Go through the trajectory step by step and check each item. Edit only where an item clearly applies.

1. Repeated work. A turn that repeats an earlier command, or checks again something the terminal already showed,
   without new information: delete it.
2. Dead ends. A turn whose output the agent never used and that the solution did not need: delete it, unless a later
   step relies on what it did or showed.
3. Wrong statements in the reasoning. A claim that contradicts the task or what the terminal showed (a misread output,
   a wrong count, a wrong belief about what a command does): correct that statement, nothing more.
4. Circling reasoning. Long back-and-forth that ends at the right decision: cut the repetition and the abandoned
   alternatives, and keep the agent's own sentences for the considerations that matter and for the decision. Do not
   summarize the reasoning in your own words.
5. Detours in actions. An action that takes an indirect or trial-and-error path when the direct one was already
   evident from what had been observed: replace it with the direct action. If the agent could not have known yet,
   leave it.
6. Fragile commands. A command that worked by luck, or does something other than what the reasoning says (a
   redirection order that loses stderr, an unquoted pattern): fix it, as long as the result the task checks stays
   the same.

## What a good rewrite leaves alone
7. Correct reasoning and correct actions, even if you would phrase or do them differently. No changes of style,
   formatting or command preference.
8. The commands that produce the task's final result, unless they are wrong. If you change one, the terminal output
   you write must be exactly what it would print.
9. The structure: do not add, merge or reorder turns.
10. Steps {protected}: they complete the task. Do not delete them or change their commands or terminal outputs; you
    may correct wrong statements in their reasoning, and in the analysis and plan of their action (set_command with
    the same commands).

## Keep it consistent
11. No hindsight. What a step reasons or does may only use the task and what the terminal had shown before that step,
    as edited.
12. After deleting or changing a step, check every later step: reasoning that refers to output that no longer exists,
    and terminal outputs that would now be different. Fix both.
13. A terminal output you write must be what the real shell would print, in the same format as the surrounding
    outputs, including the first line ("New Terminal Output:") and the shell prompt lines. Never write a success
    the commands would not produce.

## Decide
14. Small: delete or replace the action of at most {budget} of the {n} steps. If the trajectory is already clean,
    answer with no edits; when in doubt, leave a step as it is.
15. Decide whether the edited trajectory would still pass the task's tests. If it would not, answer with no edits.

## How to write the edits
- edit_thinking: "old" is a short exact quote from that step's reasoning, copied character for character (one sentence
  or less), that occurs exactly once there; "new" replaces it, and an empty "new" deletes it.
- set_command: the complete action JSON the agent writes: {"analysis": "...", "plan": "...", "commands":
  [{"keystrokes": "...\n", "duration": 0.1}], "task_complete": false}.
- set_terminal: the full terminal output after that step.
