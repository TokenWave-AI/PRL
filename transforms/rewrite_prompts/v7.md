You review a trajectory of an AI agent that solved a task in a Linux terminal: it passed the task's tests. Correct the agent's mistakes and leave everything else exactly as the agent wrote it. The result is used to train the agent in a particular way: the words you write stay in the trajectory but are not trained on; the agent is trained only on its own remaining words, in the context you corrected. So write what a correction needs, but take away as little of the agent's own text as possible: every sentence of the agent you delete or replace loses its training signal. You also play the terminal: where your edits change what the terminal would have shown, you write what it would show instead.

## What to correct
Go through the trajectory step by step. Edit only where one of these clearly applies.

1. Wrong statements in the reasoning. A claim that contradicts the task or what the terminal showed (a misread output,
   a wrong count, a wrong belief about what a command does): replace only the wrong sentence, or the wrong part of it,
   with a correct one in the agent's own words.
2. Wrong commands. A command that does something other than what the reasoning intends, or worked only by luck (a
   redirection order that loses stderr, an unquoted pattern): change only that command, as long as the result the task
   checks stays the same, and write the terminal output it would really print.

## What to leave exactly as it is
3. The agent's way of thinking: hesitation ("maybe", "I think"), self-corrections ("wait", "actually"), exploration,
   alternatives it considered, and checks of its own work, even when they look redundant. Do not delete or shorten
   reasoning that is not wrong, and do not make it sound more certain.
4. Correct reasoning and correct actions, even if you would phrase or do them differently. No changes of style,
   formatting or command preference; do not add checks or outputs the agent did not have.
5. The steps: do not add, merge or reorder turns, and do not delete a turn unless correcting it would change more.
6. Steps {protected}: they complete the task. Do not delete them or change their commands or terminal outputs; you
   may correct wrong statements in their reasoning, and in the analysis and plan of their action (set_command with
   the same commands).

## Keep it consistent
7. No hindsight. What a step reasons or does may only use the task and what the terminal had shown before that step,
   as edited.
8. After changing a step, check every later step: reasoning that refers to output that no longer exists, and terminal
   outputs that would now be different. Fix both, again with the smallest edits.
9. A terminal output you write must be what the real shell would print, in the same format as the surrounding
   outputs, including the first line ("New Terminal Output:") and the shell prompt lines. Never write a success
   the commands would not produce.

## Decide
10. Keep the agent's text. Correct each mistake where it is, replacing only the wrong words. Hard limits: at most {budget}
    of the {n} steps deleted or given a new action, and the agent's own text you delete or replace may be at most
    {removed_share} of it (what you write does not count). If nothing is wrong, answer with no edits.
11. Decide whether the edited trajectory would still pass the task's tests. If it would not, answer with no edits.

## How to write the edits
- edit_thinking: "old" is a short exact quote from that step's reasoning, copied character for character (one sentence
  or less), that occurs exactly once there; "new" replaces it.
- set_command: the complete action JSON the agent writes, with every field it had: {"analysis": "...", "plan": "...",
  "commands": [{"keystrokes": "...\n", "duration": 0.1}], "task_complete": false}.
- set_terminal: the full terminal output after that step.
